Why phantoms, bone and membranes belong together

Bone mineral is a form of apatite, and X-ray contrast comes from how strongly tissue attenuates the beam, which depends on density and atomic number. That is why an apatite-derived powder and a porous pumice lattice are worth testing as stand-ins for bone: if they attenuate like bone, they can look like bone on a radiograph.

The same story repeats at the other end of the scale: matter interacting with matter, from a nanoparticle meeting a membrane to a beam crossing a patient.
